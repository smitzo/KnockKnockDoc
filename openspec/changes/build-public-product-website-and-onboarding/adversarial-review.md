# Adversarial product and engineering review

## Decisions challenged

### “A proper website needs every marketplace feature now”

No. A broad medical-tourism marketplace would confuse the doctor-first thesis and create unsupported operational promises. The public site needs complete information architecture and honest capability boundaries before it needs booking, pricing, travel fulfillment, or patient acquisition.

### “Registration means immediate access”

No. Email ownership is not credential verification. Automatic doctor/hospital activation would allow impersonation, fraudulent profiles, data scraping, spam, and self-assigned organization privileges. Registration creates a reviewable application only.

### “Doctor ratings make discovery easier”

Only if their meaning is precise. A generic five-star badge can be interpreted as clinical competence or outcomes. The product may show attributable collaboration signals with counts and methodology, never a treatment-success claim or paid ranking.

### “Hospital facts can be self-declared”

Capacity, accreditation, facilities, and international-patient services drift over time. Every publishable fact needs provenance, review ownership, an `as of` date where relevant, and expiry/reverification behavior.

### “Thirty doctors should make the public site feel populated”

No. Admin fixtures exist to test workflows, not to manufacture public marketplace liquidity. Publishing fictional provider cards to anonymous visitors would erode trust and may mislead vulnerable patients. Public data and admin fixtures remain separate.

### “Copy the leading competitor's funnel”

No. Benchmark navigation and user questions; do not copy protected content, unsupported scale claims, ranking mechanics, WhatsApp leakage, or a patient-lead business model that conflicts with the product thesis.

## Stop-ship gaps before real applicants or public providers

- verified email delivery, token rotation, abuse monitoring, and account recovery;
- credential-review operations, reviewer training, evidence retention, appeal, and reverification;
- private upload quarantine, malware scanning, encryption, object-level access, and deletion proof;
- privacy/terms/accessibility/contact policies approved for launch jurisdictions;
- organization-domain and authorized-representative verification;
- moderation, profile correction, suspension, takedown, and incident-response operations;
- corridor-specific clinical, privacy, advertising, anti-kickback, and medical-tourism legal review.

Until these gates pass, registration and provider publication remain synthetic/prototype capabilities and the UI says so.
